# What should you expect from immunotherapy side effects?

Outline for the 10-minute script. Marks are cumulative.

## Cold open (0:00)
- Name the question: what to expect from immunotherapy side effects
- "Different from chemo," but nobody says what that means
- Promise: common vs rare, timing, treatment, when to call

## Disclaimer (0:30)
- Read the disclaimer in full

## What is immunotherapy, and why does it cause side effects? (0:45)
- Define: immune system = cells that find and clear germs, damaged cells
- Define: immunotherapy = treatment using your own immune system [S1]
- Define: checkpoints = built-in brakes; cancer uses them to switch cells off [S1]
- Checkpoint inhibitor blocks the off signal [S1]
- Drugs: pembrolizumab, nivolumab, atezolizumab, ipilimumab; infusion or under skin [S4]
- Same released brake can attack healthy parts of you [S4]
- Immune-related; different from chemotherapy side effects [S6]
- Define: chemotherapy = drugs that kill fast-dividing cells
- Define: inflammation = immune system attacking part of you; any organ [S1]
- Cannot predict who, when, or how severe [S1]

## Which side effects are common, and which are rare? (2:30)
- Common three: tiredness, rash or itching, diarrhea [S1]
- Also cough, nausea, poor appetite, constipation, aches [S4]
- Skin changes common, very few serious; mild infusion reactions [S5]
- Define: melanoma = a skin cancer
- Melanoma study, 387 people: 7 in 10 affected, 9 in 10 mild [S3]
- Unlikely to get everything on the list [S9]
- More likely with two drugs, or with chemo [S5]
- Rare: lungs, bowel, liver, hormone glands, heart, kidneys, nerves [S1]
- Pembrolizumab: lung 3 in 100, liver under 1 in 100 [S10]
- Rare but serious if untreated; catch early [S5]
- 112 trials: deaths about 4 to 12 in 1,000 [S15]

## When do side effects start, and how long do they last? (4:15)
- Most in first weeks or months [S7]
- Skin first several weeks; diarrhea first six to eight weeks [S5]
- No safe window: any time, even a year after [S9] [S10] [S11]
- Blood tests: thyroid, liver, kidneys [S10]
- Bowel symptoms often normal in two to four weeks [S7]
- Study: 4 in 10 still affected at three months, mostly mild [S3]
- Hormone glands linger most: thyroid, adrenal [S3]
- Define: cortisol = stress hormone; replacement pills, sometimes for life [S5]
- Joint pain, eye problems can linger [S3]

## What happens if you get a side effect? Will treatment stop? (5:45)
- Team grades severity using written guidelines [S13] [S14]
- Mild: cream, pill, monitoring; treatment continues [S5]
- Moderate or severe: pause; pause usually stops it [S5]
- Define: steroids = medicines that calm the immune system [S5]
- Pill, IV, cream, drops; start early; taper slowly [S5]
- Misconception: steroids cancel immunotherapy; untreated is the danger [S5]
- Restart: team weighs response, risks, benefits with you [S5]
- Restart after bowel inflammation: standard care at one center [S7]
- Life-threatening: usually stopped for good [S10]
- Side effect is not failure; none is not failure [S7] [S1]

## What you can do (7:30)
- 24-hour number; wallet card shown to every clinician [S5] [S9]
- Agree what to call about [S11]:
  - three-plus watery stools, blood; fever 100.4 F
  - cough, breathlessness; yellow skin; belly pain; vision; confusion
- Chest pain or trouble breathing: emergency call [S9]
- No anti-diarrhea medicine, supplements, new medicines without asking [S11]
- Keep a short daily note

## Recap (8:45)
- Released brake causes the side effects [S1]
- Common: tiredness, rash, diarrhea; most mild, treatable [S1] [S5]
- Rare: lungs, liver, hormone glands, heart; catch early [S5]
- Can start early or long after [S5] [S11]
- Pause plus steroids; many restart [S5]
- Call the day something changes [S6]

## Talk to your oncologist (9:30)
- Question to bring: likely side effects, what to call about, which number
- Close: answer on the fridge; sorting mild from serious is their job
