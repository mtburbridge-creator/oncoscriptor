# Sources: What to expect from immunotherapy side effects
Searched 2026-10-09 by Claude.

## S1
- Title: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors
- Organization: National Cancer Institute (cancer.gov)
- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/immunotherapy/checkpoint-inhibitors
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Explains checkpoints as the immune system's normal brakes, how tumors use them to switch T cells off, and how checkpoint inhibitors (CTLA-4, PD-1, PD-L1) block that signal. Reviewed April 7, 2022.
  Common side effects are rash, diarrhea, and fatigue; rarer ones are widespread inflammation of skin, lungs, colon, pancreas, liver, pituitary, heart muscle, kidneys, thyroid, or nerves. Side effects depend on health, cancer type and stage, drug, and dose, and cannot be reliably predicted.

## S2
- Title: Immunotherapy Side Effects
- Organization: National Cancer Institute (cancer.gov)
- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/types/immunotherapy/side-effects
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: General page on side effects across all immunotherapy types; says they occur when the boosted immune system also attacks healthy cells. Reviewed February 16, 2023.
  Side effects can occur at any point during or after treatment; severe or fatal inflammation-related reactions are described as rare; patients are told to ask their team which signs to watch for.

## S3
- Title: Long-Term Side Effects of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (Cancer Currents blog, reporting a JAMA Oncology study)
- Organization: National Cancer Institute (cancer.gov)
- URL: https://www.cancer.gov/news-events/cancer-currents-blog/2021/immune-checkpoint-inhibitors-melanoma-long-term-side-effects
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Reports a real-world study of 387 people with melanoma treated with nivolumab or pembrolizumab after surgery at eight hospitals, median follow-up about 18 months. Posted April 30, 2021.
  69 percent had an immune-related side effect during treatment (87 percent of those mild); 43 percent had one lasting at least 3 months after treatment ended, 96 percent of those mild; most common chronic effects were rash, underactive thyroid, and joint pain; about a quarter stopped treatment early because of a side effect.

## S4
- Title: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors and Their Side Effects
- Organization: American Cancer Society (cancer.org)
- URL: https://www.cancer.org/cancer/treatment-types/immunotherapy/immune-checkpoint-inhibitors.html
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Patient explainer of PD-1, PD-L1, CTLA-4, and LAG-3 inhibitors with drug names, how they are given, and side effects. Last revised December 15, 2025.
  Common side effects listed: diarrhea, fatigue, cough, nausea and vomiting, skin rash, poor appetite, constipation, muscle and joint pain; infusion reactions and autoimmune reactions in lungs, intestines, liver, hormone glands, kidneys can be serious or life-threatening; serious effects may need stopping treatment and high-dose corticosteroids.

## S5
- Title: NCCN Guidelines for Patients: Immunotherapy Side Effects: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors, 2026
- Organization: National Comprehensive Cancer Network (nccn.org), based on NCCN Guidelines for Management of Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor-Related Toxicities, Version 1.2026 (October 23, 2025)
- URL: https://www.nccn.org/patients/guidelines/content/PDF/immunotherapy-checkpoint-patient.pdf
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Full patient guide, organized by body system, covering what to know before treatment, steroids and other treatments, and each organ's side effects and management.
  Side effects may start shortly after starting, within the first couple of months, or after treatment is finished; many are mild and treatable but some rare serious ones can lead to death especially if untreated; some are chronic; risk is higher with more than one immunotherapy or with chemotherapy; skin symptoms usually start in the first several weeks and diarrhea often in the first 6 to 8 weeks; pausing treatment usually stops the side effect; restarting after a severe side effect is a risk-benefit decision with the care team; steroids work best when started early and are tapered slowly; carry an immunotherapy wallet card.

## S6
- Title: Immunotherapy Side Effects: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors (infographic)
- Organization: National Comprehensive Cancer Network (nccn.org)
- URL: https://www.nccn.org/docs/default-source/patient-resources/immunotherapy_infographic.pdf?sfvrsn=f92249ca_2
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: One-page summary of warning signs by body part (pituitary, eyes, thyroid, lungs, skin, nerves, brain, mouth, heart, stomach and bowel, joints, muscles). Dated 2026.
  Reporting symptoms when they start helps find the cause early and prevent severe side effects; side effects may appear within the first couple of months or after finishing therapy; many are mild and can be treated, often with steroids; feeling tired is common.

## S7
- Title: Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Side Effects
- Organization: MD Anderson Cancer Center (mdanderson.org)
- URL: https://www.mdanderson.org/patients-family/diagnosis-treatment/emotional-physical-effects/immune-checkpoint-inhibitor-side-effects.html
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Patient page on why side effects happen, infusion reactions, short-term and longer-term effects by organ, risk factors, timing, management, and when to go to the emergency room. Page shows copyright 2026, no article date.
  Most side effects appear within the first few weeks or months, rarely months or years later; more than 60 percent of patients receiving some form of immunotherapy develop side effects; a side effect does not necessarily mean treatment is not working; GI symptoms often return to baseline in two to four weeks and resuming treatment after colitis is described as standard care; emergency thresholds include more than seven bowel movements a day, blood or black stools, shortness of breath at rest, chest pain, rash over more than half the body.

## S8
- Title: Checkpoint inhibitors
- Organization: Cancer Research UK (cancerresearchuk.org)
- URL: https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/treatment/targeted-cancer-drugs-immunotherapy/checkpoint-inhibitors
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Explains how checkpoint inhibitors release the brakes on T cells, lists drugs by target, and lists side effects. Last reviewed 28 October 2024.
  Common side effects include skin rashes and itching, diarrhoea, tiredness, nausea, loss of appetite, breathlessness and dry cough from lung inflammation; the liver, kidneys, and hormone glands such as the thyroid can be affected, which is why regular blood tests are done; side effects can occur during treatment or several months after it finishes and in some people can be life threatening.

## S9
- Title: What is immunotherapy and how is it given?
- Organization: Macmillan Cancer Support (macmillan.org.uk)
- URL: https://www.macmillan.org.uk/cancer-information-and-support/treatment/types-of-treatment/immunotherapy
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Patient overview of immunotherapy, its immune-related side effects, and practical safety advice. Reviewed 1 May 2024.
  You are unlikely to get all the side effects listed; side effects can start mild and become serious without treatment, and can begin during treatment or weeks, months, or more than a year after it ends; save the 24-hour hospital numbers and call any time you feel unwell; carry the alert card and show it to any health professional; chest pain or difficulty breathing needs an emergency call.

## S10
- Title: KEYTRUDA (pembrolizumab) prescribing information
- Organization: Merck, via DailyMed, U.S. National Library of Medicine (dailymed.nlm.nih.gov); FDA-approved label
- URL: https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=9333c79b-d487-4538-a9f0-71b91a02b287
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: FDA label for one widely used PD-1 inhibitor. Warnings section 5.1 lists immune-mediated pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, endocrinopathies, nephritis, dermatologic reactions, and others including myocarditis and neurologic toxicities. Revised July 2026.
  In 2,799 patients: pneumonitis 3.4 percent, colitis 1.7 percent, hepatitis 0.7 percent, hypothyroidism 8 percent, hyperthyroidism 3.4 percent, adrenal insufficiency 0.8 percent, hypophysitis 0.6 percent, type 1 diabetes 0.2 percent, nephritis 0.3 percent, severe skin reactions 1.4 percent; reactions can occur at any time after starting and after stopping; in general the drug is withheld for Grade 3 reactions and permanently discontinued for Grade 4; liver, kidney, and thyroid blood tests are checked before and during treatment.

## S11
- Title: Managing Your Immunotherapy Side Effects
- Organization: Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center (mskcc.org)
- URL: https://www.mskcc.org/cancer-care/patient-education/managing-your-immunotherapy-side-effects
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Patient education sheet with symptom lists per side effect and specific "call your provider" thresholds. Last updated May 31, 2023.
  Immune-related side effects can start right away or up to 2 years after treatment ends; call for more than 3 watery stools in a day or any blood in stool; call right away for new or worsening cough, shortness of breath, yellow skin or eyes, severe belly pain, vision changes, persistent headaches, extreme tiredness, confusion; call for a fever of 100.4 F (38 C) or higher; do not take diarrhea medicine without asking first; tell the team before starting any new medicine, steroid, supplement, or herbal remedy.

## S15
- Title: Fatal Toxic Effects Associated With Immune Checkpoint Inhibitors: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis
- Organization: JAMA Oncology (Wang DY et al.), 2018;4(12):1721-1728, PMID 30242316, DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2018.3923
- URL: https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:30242316%20AND%20SRC:MED&format=json
- Date accessed: 2026-10-09
- Summary: Systematic review and meta-analysis of 112 trials with 19,217 patients plus a pharmacovigilance database, from research/literature.md; abstract read through Europe PMC.
  Toxicity-related fatality rates in the trials ranged from 0.36 percent to 1.23 percent depending on the regimen; deaths tended to occur early after treatment began; myocarditis had the highest fatality rate among reported cases.
