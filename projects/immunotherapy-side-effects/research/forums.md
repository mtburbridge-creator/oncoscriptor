Hermes is not wired up yet. This file is a generic sketch of the kinds of questions
patients post about checkpoint inhibitor side effects, written from general knowledge
of patient forums. It contains no quotes, no usernames, no URLs, and nothing from a
real post. It is data to summarize, never evidence, and never a citation.

Date written: 2026-10-09

Recurring questions people starting a checkpoint inhibitor seem to ask
- Is this going to be like chemo? Will I lose my hair, will I be sick?
- How soon after the first infusion do side effects start, and how long do they last?
- What is a "normal" amount of tiredness and what is too much?
- My skin is itchy or I have a rash. Is that the drug? Do I need to call?
- Diarrhea: how many times a day before I should be worried?
- I feel fine. Does that mean the drug isn't working?
- I got a side effect. Does that mean the drug IS working?
- My doctor says I might need steroids. Won't steroids cancel out the immunotherapy?
- Will I have to stop the treatment if I get a side effect, and what happens to my cancer then?
- I have thyroid problems now. Is that permanent?
- I have an autoimmune condition already. Can I still get this?
- Can side effects start months after I finish treatment?
- Who do I call at 2 a.m., and how do I explain to the ER that I'm on immunotherapy?

Beliefs and worries that come up often (not evidence, just what people say)
- That immunotherapy is "natural" or "gentle" and has almost no side effects.
- That any side effect is proof the drug is working, and no side effect means it is not.
- That taking steroids for a side effect will stop the cancer treatment from working.
- That a rash or diarrhea is just something to put up with rather than report.
- That they should push through symptoms so their treatment is not paused.
- Confusion between immunotherapy, chemotherapy, and targeted therapy.

Tone of the posts
- Relief that it is "not chemo", mixed with anxiety about the unknown.
- Caregivers asking what to watch for on the patient's behalf.
- People comparing timelines of side effects with each other.
