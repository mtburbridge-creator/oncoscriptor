# News: What to expect from immunotherapy side effects
Searched 2026-10-09 by Claude. Window: 2025-04-09 to 2026-10-09.

## N1
- Headline: Corticosteroids used for cancer symptom relief may limit benefit of some immunotherapies
- Outlet: Healio (HemOnc Today)
- URL: https://www.healio.com/news/hematology-oncology/20250814/corticosteroids-used-for-cancer-symptom-relief-may-limit-benefit-of-some-immunotherapies
- Date published: 2025-08-14
- What it says: Retrospective study of 277 people with stage II to IV non-small cell lung cancer on checkpoint inhibitors; the 21 (8 percent) already taking steroids when they started had a much lower response rate and shorter survival.
  The finding is about steroids already being taken at the start of treatment; the article cites an earlier review that steroids used to treat immune-related side effects had no impact on outcomes.
- What a patient might take from it: "Steroids cancel out my immunotherapy", and a temptation to refuse or stop steroids prescribed for a side effect, which the article itself warns against.

## N2
- Headline: Corticosteroids May Limit Efficacy of Immunotherapy in Patients With NSCLC
- Outlet: The ASCO Post
- URL: https://ascopost.com/news/july-2025/corticosteroids-may-limit-efficacy-of-immunotherapy-in-patients-with-nsclc/
- Date published: 2025-07-09
- What it says: Same Polyakov et al. study; people who had high-dose steroids before or during checkpoint inhibitor therapy had less tumor shrinkage and shorter survival, and a mouse experiment suggested steroids keep T cells from maturing.
  Observational, and the article does not separate baseline steroids from steroids given for immune-related side effects.
- What a patient might take from it: That any steroid use will make treatment fail; the article notes steroids remain necessary for some people and says to discuss with the oncologist.

## N3
- Headline: Keytruda Qlex: FDA Approves New Subcutaneous Immune Therapy Shot for Treating a Wide Range of Advanced Cancers
- Outlet: WebMD
- URL: https://www.webmd.com/drugs/updates/keytruda-qlex-fda-approves-subcutaneous-immune-therapy-shot
- Date published: medically reviewed 2025-09-22 (no publication date shown)
- What it says: An under-the-skin form of pembrolizumab, given in one to two minutes instead of a 30-minute infusion, approved for most cancers the IV form treats, based on a 377-patient lung cancer study with similar response rates.
  Side effects are described as largely similar to the IV form, including tiredness, rash, diarrhea, and thyroid problems, with the same serious immune-related reactions possible during or after treatment.
- What a patient might take from it: That the shot is a gentler version with fewer side effects; the article says the side-effect profile is essentially the same.

## N4
- Headline: Early Myocarditis Onset After Immunotherapy May Predict Treatment-related Fatality
- Outlet: American Association for Cancer Research, press release (AACR Annual Meeting 2026)
- URL: https://www.aacr.org/about-the-aacr/newsroom/news-releases/early-myocarditis-onset-after-immunotherapy-may-predict-treatment-related-fatality/
- Date published: 2026-04-21
- What it says: From the WHO VigiBase reporting database, 2,641 cases of checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis; myocarditis starting within the first month was linked to higher likelihood of dying from it, highest (38 percent) when myositis and myasthenia gravis occurred together.
  Retrospective and descriptive; the database lacked complete treatment information; a prediction tool is still being validated.
- What a patient might take from it: That heart inflammation is common or that a 38 percent death rate applies to them; myocarditis itself is rare and the figure is among reported cases only.

## N5
- Headline: Early myocarditis onset after immunotherapy linked to higher fatality risk
- Outlet: News-Medical
- URL: https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260421/Early-myocarditis-onset-after-immunotherapy-linked-to-higher-fatality-risk.aspx
- Date published: 2026-04-21
- What it says: Same AACR abstract; first-month onset linked to higher fatality than later onset, 38 percent with the triple overlap versus roughly 21 to 26 percent in other groups.
  Relative-risk language ("59 percent more likely") without absolute risk.
- What a patient might take from it: Relative increases read as personal odds; the piece is about a rare event among reported cases.

## N6
- Headline: Early-Onset ICI-Related Myocarditis Linked to Increased Mortality
- Outlet: The ASCO Post
- URL: https://ascopost.com/news/april-2026/early-onset-ici-related-myocarditis-linked-to-increased-mortality/
- Date published: 2026-04-22 (updated 2026-05-04)
- What it says: Same abstract with the same numbers; deaths 38 percent with triple-M overlap versus 21.2 percent with myocarditis alone.
  Conference abstract from a reporting database; the prediction model was validated on only 28 external cases.
- What a patient might take from it: That chest symptoms early in treatment are a death sentence rather than a reason to call the same day.

## N7
- Headline: What Patients Should Know About Lifelong Endocrine Side Effects of Immunotherapy
- Outlet: CURE
- URL: https://www.curetoday.com/view/what-patients-should-know-about-lifelong-endocrine-adverse-effects-of-immunotherapy
- Date published: 2026-05-16
- What it says: Summary of an Oncology Nursing Society Congress talk: checkpoint inhibitors can affect the thyroid, adrenal glands, pituitary, and pancreas, and some of these (adrenal insufficiency, diabetes) can be permanent and need lifelong hormone or insulin replacement.
  No incidence numbers given; calls some endocrine complications rare.
- What a patient might take from it: That most people end up on lifelong hormone pills; the article has no rates and is a talk summary.

## N8
- Headline: Side effects of immunotherapy injections for cancer
- Outlet: Medical News Today
- URL: https://www.medicalnewstoday.com/articles/side-effects-subcutaneous-immunotherapy-cancer
- Date published: updated 2026-06-15
- What it says: Injected and IV checkpoint inhibitors work the same way and have the same range of short- and long-term side effects, including immune-related reactions in any body system.
  Does not claim fewer or milder side effects with the injection.
- What a patient might take from it: That a shot means fewer side effects; it does not.

## N9
- Headline: Combination immunotherapy for rare cancers shows improved efficacy and survival rates
- Outlet: Medical Xpress
- URL: https://medicalxpress.com/news/2025-11-combination-immunotherapy-rare-cancers-efficacy.html
- Date published: 2025-11-14
- What it says: Trial of nivolumab plus ipilimumab in mismatch-repair-deficient cancers with a 63 percent response rate.
  Reports no side-effect or toxicity rates at all for the combination.
- What a patient might take from it: That combination immunotherapy is a free upgrade; trusted sources say side effects are more likely with more than one immunotherapy, which this coverage omits.

## Older context
- The ASCO Post, 2025-04-01 (eight days before the window): "Some Patients With Advanced NSCLC Experience Durable Disease Control After Immunotherapy Discontinuation", https://ascopost.com/news/april-2025/some-patients-with-advanced-nsclc-experience-durable-disease-control-after-immunotherapy-discontinuation/ . Of 2,794 people, 271 (10 percent) stopped immunotherapy because of immune-related side effects, and a subset kept disease control for a long time afterward (median 12.7 months progression-free). Retrospective; a reader might take it as a promise that stopping is safe.
- CURE, 2023-08-08: "Immunotherapy May Lead to Chronic Side Effects", https://www.curetoday.com/view/immunotherapy-may-lead-to-chronic-side-effects . Among 318 people with melanoma on adjuvant nivolumab or pembrolizumab, 46.2 percent had a chronic immune-related side effect lasting 3 months or longer; about a third resolved by 34.7 months; 32.4 percent of those retreated had a flare. The NCI blog version of this story is already S3 in sources.md.

## Themes
- Steroids versus immunotherapy: two outlets covered the same retrospective lung study (N1, N2) with headlines suggesting steroids blunt immunotherapy. Patients will ask whether steroids for a side effect undo the treatment. The story is about steroids taken at baseline, not steroids for side effects.
- Heart inflammation: the AACR myocarditis abstract got broad coverage (N4, N5, N6) with fatality percentages. Patients will arrive frightened about heart risk and need the rarity put in proportion and the "call the same day" message.
- The new shot: subcutaneous pembrolizumab coverage (N3, N8) may make people expect a gentler drug; side effects are the same.
- Side effects that last: lifelong endocrine effects (N7) and the chronic-effects story (older context) raise "is this permanent?" questions.

## Claims that need a trusted source
- Steroids given for an immune-related side effect do not reduce the cancer benefit (N1 cites a review saying no impact) -> no trusted patient source in sources.md states this directly; see Gaps in sources-notes.md. The script should not assert it either way.
- Myocarditis is rare (N4, N5, N6) -> S5 (rare, can come on quickly), S1 (rarer side effects include heart muscle inflammation).
- Myocarditis fatality 21 to 38 percent among reported cases (N4, N5, N6) -> no trusted patient source gives a fatality rate; do not use the number in the script.
- Injected and IV forms have the same side effects (N3, N8) -> S4 says most are given by IV and some under the skin; the "same side effects" claim has no S key, so phrase only that both forms exist.
- Some endocrine effects can be permanent and need lifelong replacement (N7) -> S5 (hydrocortisone for life for hypophysitis; diabetes may need management for life; some side effects are chronic), S3 (thyroid and adrenal problems most likely to persist).
- Combination immunotherapy raises the chance of side effects (implied by N9's omission) -> S5.
- Some people keep disease control after stopping for side effects (older context) -> no trusted patient source; the script should stick to S5's "restarting is a risk-benefit discussion".
